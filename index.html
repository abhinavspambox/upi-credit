Project 02 · Payments strategy · NPCI UPI statistics, Jan–Oct 2026

Everyday spending lives on the QR code. Where does that leave the credit card?

UPI keeps setting records while its average payment gets smaller. Credit cards win the large purchases; UPI wins the daily habit. This project measures the split and designs a product that lets a premium issuer take part in small-ticket spending.

What the data shows

  • Frequency belongs to UPI. Monthly volume rose 17.4% from January to October 2026, from 21.70 to 25.47 billion transactions. Value grew more slowly, 11.3%.
  • Payments are getting smaller. The average UPI payment fell from ₹1,306 in January to ₹1,238 in October, as everyday purchases move to QR.
  • The value gap is large. In every month from January to September 2026, UPI moved 13.5 to 15.2 times the value of all credit card spending.
  • Credit is already arriving on UPI. RuPay credit cards on UPI carried about 38% of credit card transactions by volume but only around 8% by value (Bernstein, October 2025). Only RuPay credit cards can be linked to UPI today.
  • Share is shifting. UPI's share of digital merchant payment value rose to 77.3% in July 2026 from 74.9% a year earlier, while credit cards slipped to 17.7% from 19.8% (Equirus).

The user problem

Persona: Rohan, 34, consultant, Gurugram. He holds a premium travel card on a global network for flights and hotels. Every day he also makes ten or more small payments by scanning QR codes from his savings account. Those payments earn no rewards, don't use his credit limit and never show up for his card issuer.

For a premium issuer, Rohan's daily habit is invisible. The issuer sees a few large transactions a month and loses the frequency that builds loyalty.

The product: a "credit on QR" companion

Companion RuPay creditA virtual RuPay credit card issued on the same credit limit as the primary card, linked to UPI in two taps.RuPay is the only credit network allowed on UPI.
Small-spend rewardsFlat points on QR payments, capped each month, that pool into the primary card's rewards.Builds frequency without unlimited reward cost.
One statementPrimary and companion spends on one bill, one due date, one app view.No extra complexity for the customer.
Spend controlsPer-transaction and monthly caps for QR payments, set by the customer.Reduces fraud exposure and over-spending.

As a premium cardholder, I want my daily QR payments to earn the same rewards as my card, so that I don't have to choose between convenience and value.

The economics to test

  • RuPay credit card payments on UPI under ₹2,000 at smaller merchants carry no merchant discount rate, so the issuer earns little from each small payment.
  • The case rests on second-order value: higher engagement, more revolving balances from customers who choose to revolve, and lower churn of high-value customers.
  • NPCI deferred a planned merchant charge on select UPI transactions until after Diwali 2026. Any change in UPI merchant pricing changes this model, so I would build it to update with fee assumptions.

Success metrics

  • North star: monthly active days per customer across both cards.
  • Input metrics: companion activation rate, QR transactions per active companion, share of customer's monthly spend on the issuer.
  • Guardrails: reward cost per transaction, fraud loss rate, delinquency on companion spend.

Risks and trade-offs

  • A second card can confuse customers. The companion must stay invisible except in the UPI app.
  • Network partnerships take time. An issuer without RuPay issuance needs a partner bank or a co-brand.
  • Rewards on tiny payments can cost more than they earn. Caps and a points floor protect the unit economics.

Discovery plan

The proposal assumes premium cardholders want rewards on small QR payments. That is the riskiest belief, so I would test it first, with 8 people who hold a premium card and use UPI daily.

Method

Monthly UPI volume and value for January–October 2026 come from NPCI releases as reported in the press. I derived average ticket size and the ratio of UPI value to RBI-reported card spend. The data (each row with its source link) and the script are in this folder; the SQL version is in the data workbench.
